Overview

About This Service

In simple wordsA literature review is a summary of what other researchers have already found about your topic.

Before you do new research, you need to know what others have done. We find the important studies, read them, and group them by idea.

Then we show where the studies agree, where they disagree, and what is still unknown. This gap is often where your own research begins.

Deliverables

What You Get

Source search

Relevant, recent, peer-reviewed sources on your topic.

Thematic structure

Sources grouped by theme, method, or debate instead of listed one by one.

Critical comparison

Strengths, weaknesses, and disagreements between studies.

Research gap

A clear statement of what the literature hasn't answered yet.

Is it for you?

Who It's For

  • Students writing a thesis or dissertation

  • Students preparing a research proposal

  • Anyone who needs a standalone review

Simple process

How It Works

  1. 1

    Step 1: Set the scope

    Send your topic, question, date range, and any required sources.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Search the literature

    We find relevant peer-reviewed studies in academic databases.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Group and compare

    We organize the sources by theme and compare their findings.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Identify the gap

    We write the review, ending with what the research hasn't answered yet.

Before we start

What to Send Us

  • Your topic and research question
  • Required sources or databases, if any
  • Date range or number of sources
  • Citation style, length, and deadline

Good to know

Questions

How many sources will you use?

It depends on your topic and length. Tell us if your course sets a minimum.

Can you use the sources I already found?

Yes. Send them and we'll build on them.
